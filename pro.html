The decks

Built to feel like hardware

Native C++ from the audio engine to the interface - no web page in a window - so it stays tight under load.

4

Four decks, two sides

A layer switch per side, so a two-channel controller drives all four. Play, cue, sync, key-locked tempo, slip, vinyl mode, hot cues and loops on every deck.

↻

A platter with real momentum

A modelled motor spins up and down, so backspins, stalls and hand-brakes behave like vinyl. Scratching runs on the audio thread and holds up under load.

CDJ

CDJ mode

With scratch off, the jog bends the pitch for as long as it turns and a hand on top never stops the music. Tempo Sync keeps a synced deck's tempo while you nudge the beat.

1234

Grids on the drums

Every analysed track gets a beat grid placed on the kicks, numbered 1 2 3 4 on the waveform. Nudge it from the keyboard; the nudge is saved with the track.

BPM

Readable numbers

Whole BPMs as they play, the key, and waveforms that stretch with the tempo - two decks at the same BPM draw their beats the same distance apart.

♫

Now Playing

The track the room is hearing - artwork, title, artist, BPM, key and its spectrum - big enough to read on a stream or in a video, and steady through a blend.

Stems in the box

Bass, vocals, drums - on three faders

Demucs and a GPU-ready PyTorch come with the app: tracks are split into four stems on your own PC, using an NVIDIA graphics card when there is one. Nothing is uploaded.

  • The three EQ faders drive the stems instead of filters: low is the bass, mid the vocals, high the drums.
  • Stems are finished before they're saved - they add back up to the track, and the bass keeps all of its low end, so cutting it takes the whole bassline.
  • The Night Shift can work through your library in the background, making Best stems from two separators.
  • Rather not split anything? Switch the mixer to EQ in Settings.

The smart fader

The crossfader steers the basslines too, so two tracks' bass never stack - and a deck faded out leaves an echo behind it.

Harvest a sample

Pull a sample straight out of a stem into one of the sampler's banks.

FX and pads

Effects that go in and out clean

No clicks, no holes, no jumps in level when an effect comes in or goes out - and echoes and reverbs ring on and die away by themselves.

Six paddle FX

HPF, LPF, flanger, phaser, echo and reverb on the paddles, on the beat - the filter knob is the cutoff, the reverb a real convolution room.

Ten pad FX

Echo, dub echo, space echo, reverb, flanger, phaser, filter sweep, crush, auto filter and master tempo - held on the pads. A panic button ends every tail at once.

Eight pad modes

Hot cues, auto loops, rolls, a slicer, the sampler, fader cuts, pitch play and scratch banks. Rolls and the slicer slip: let go and the track is where it would have been.

Colour FX

One colour FX knob per channel, the effect stepped from the controller, with its own kill.

Sampler

Five banks - vocals, horns, bass, drums and deck FX - remembered from one launch to the next.

Auto DJ and Auto Blend

Let it mix for you, or hand it one blend at a time.

Controllers and audio

Your gear, your way

  • Built against the Numark NS4FX - its screens, its lights in Serato's layout, its Shift layer and its assign switches.
  • Launchpad support, with per-deck pad quadrants.
  • Any MIDI controller: a mapper with 61 actions in 10 categories, learned from a touch of the control.
  • Controller detection: the app asks a controller who it is the moment it's plugged in.
  • Headphones on any output - even a different device from the speakers, kept in time with them.
  • A mic input that ducks the music, and knows when it's only hearing the app's own output coming back.

What you'll need

System requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.
  • About 5 GB of disk space (a 2 GB download) - most of it is the stem separator.
  • An NVIDIA graphics card makes stem separation much faster; without one it runs on the processor.
  • A DJ controller is optional: keyboard and mouse work too.
  • The internet only for updates, the stem model's first download, and the assistant if you want it.
Is it really free?

Yes. No subscription, no account, no trial, nothing locked. Download it and use all of it.

Windows warns me about the installer

The installer isn't signed with a paid code-signing certificate yet, so SmartScreen may say it's from an unknown publisher. Choose More info, then Run anyway. It installs for you alone, without admin rights.

Why is the download 2 GB?

Stem separation runs on your own PC, so the app carries PyTorch, Demucs and NVIDIA's GPU libraries with it. Updates after that only download what changed.

It won't start

Install Microsoft's Visual C++ Redistributable (x64) and try again - most PCs have it already. If it still won't, please tell me on the feedback page.

Where are my library and settings kept?

Apart from the app, in your own profile, so updating or reinstalling never touches them.
